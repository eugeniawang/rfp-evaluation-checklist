p3:6|1.1.       CM SERVICES SCOPE OF WORK
p3:7|CDOT is soliciting Construction Manager services for the US50 SHIFT Passing Lanes project. This
p3:8|project includes roadway widening for twelve passing lanes between MP 317.5 & MP 467.5 on US50B.
p3:9|The major features of widening work include embankment, unclassified excavation, paving, resurfacing,
p3:10|guardrail, drainage features, utility coordination/relocation, signage and pavement markings. The
p3:11|Successful Proposer (also referred to as “Construction Manager (CM))”, will provide CM services for the
p3:12|preconstruction phase and will have the opportunity to negotiate a price to complete the construction of
p3:13|any package(s). If negotiation is successful, the CM will become the General Contractor (GC) with
p3:14|CDOT for the construction of that package. The GC Construction Project Contract award to the CM is not
p3:15|guaranteed but is contingent on a successful negotiation of a Construction Agreed Price (CAP).
p3:16|The Project Scope Elements are described in Section 1.3.B of this Request for Proposal (RFP). The
p3:17|Project Scope Elements may be modified based on packaging, CM input, Stakeholder input, and final
p3:18|design refinements.
p3:19|The scope of work reflects an approach based on the Project Goals and known risks. A primary benefit of
p3:20|CM/GC is the ability to contractually allocate risks to the party best able to manage the risk. Risk
p3:21|assessment will be a continual process throughout the preconstruction and construction phase of the
p3:22|project. The process will incorporate risk sharing between public and private parties holding both parties
p3:23|accountable for performance and expenditure of public resources.
p3:24|The CM shall analyze the Project Goals, evaluate work elements, identify risks, quantify risk, and
p3:25|mitigate risks. The CM shall also articulate a clear, well thought out plan for delivering the Project on
p3:26|time and on budget with accountability of public resources throughout the process. The CM shall consider
p3:27|new approaches, innovation, modifying the Project work elements, the Project sequencing, and/or the
p3:28|Project packaging to increase the efficiency of the Project delivery.
p3:29|The CM will be part of an integrated design team (Design Team) which will consist of CDOT, the Design
p3:30|Consultant, and the Independent Cost Estimator (ICE). The CM will provide input on schedule, phasing,
p3:31|constructability, quality assurance of the design, and project cost estimating throughout the
p3:32|preconstruction phase of the Project as well as general support services to ensure complete and efficient
p3:33|scoping of the different Project Elements. The CM will offer innovative ideas and risk mitigation
p3:34|measures throughout the preconstruction phase to proactively assist the Design Team. In addition to the
p3:35|base input expected of the CM, the CM should also provide opportunities, means, and methods to protect
p3:36|the traveling public and reduce the construction duration to minimize impacts to traveling public and
p3:37|reduce costs.
p3:38|If it is determined to be in the interest of CDOT, CDOT may forgo negotiations of any portion of the
p3:39|project and advertise/procure those portions of the Project separately. If this happens, the CM's Firm will
p3:40|not be eligible to submit a bid for any package, advertised or procured separately, in which the CM’s Firm
p3:41|participated in the preconstruction activities.
p3:42|The CM’s tasks during the preconstruction phase include, but are not limited to:
p3:43|       A. Design Review:
p3:44|          ●   Thorough review of all plans, specifications, reports, diagrams, shop drawings, as-built plans,
p3:45|              site conditions, and all other necessary Project documentation to provide design validation
p3:46|              from a construction expertise perspective.

p8:8|D. Risk:
p8:9|   Risk is defined as an uncertain event or condition that, if it occurs, has an impact, either positive
p8:10|   or negative, on a project’s goals and objectives. The CM/GC delivery method provides a forum to
p8:11|   communicate and discuss risk in the preconstruction phase and to collaboratively address and
p8:12|   reduce risk with the Owner, CM, and the Design Consultant. A primary benefit of CM/GC is the
p8:13|   ability to contractually allocate risks to the party best able to manage the risk. Risk assessment
p8:14|   will be a continual process throughout the preconstruction and construction phases with risk
p8:15|   sharing between public and private parties that holds both accountable for performance and
p8:16|   expenditure of public resources.
p8:17|   Risk management will be a topic at both the 2-day Kickoff meeting and an initial Risk
p8:18|   Management and Assessment Workshop. The workshop shall be scheduled by the CM early in
p8:19|   the preconstruction phase. Regular risk meetings, facilitated by the CM, will be held to monitor
p8:20|   progress. Risk responsibilities include:
p8:21|   ●   Facilitate quantitative and qualitative risk management discussions to identify risks, quantify
p8:22|       probabilities, quantify impacts, develop mitigation strategies, and assign risk responsibility.
p8:23|   ●   Set risk meeting frequencies and prepare and update the Project Risk Matrix throughout the
p8:24|       Project lifecycle.
p8:25|   ●   Collaborate with the Project Team to develop a Risk Management Plan, perform risk
p8:26|       assessments, and prepare and update the Risk Matrix.
p8:27|
p8:28|   ●   Finalize Risk Register, including narratives, to be submitted with CAP estimates.

p11:40|1.3.       PROJECT DESCRIPTION/SCOPE OF WORK
p11:41|       A. Project Background
p11:42|          The US 50 SHIFT Passing Lanes project is located along the US50B High Plains Freight
p11:43|          Corridor, which is part of the National Highway Freight Network. The project focuses on
p11:44|          providing passing lanes in stretches of two-way head-to-head highway conditions. The corridor is
p11:45|          a heavy freight and agriculture route, and construction must accommodate large trucks and
p11:46|          oversized loads. This project aims to increase efficiency and reliability and improve overall safety
p11:47|          along the corridor. The status of the project can be found at the following link:
p11:48|          https://www.codot.gov/projects/studies/us50passinglanes

p12:7|   B. Project Information and Definition
p12:8|       The goal of the Project is to construct the Project scope elements below in their entirety while
p12:9|       minimizing impacts to the traveling public and other stakeholders. The Project Scope Elements
p12:10|       may be modified based on packaging, CM input, Stakeholder input, and final design refinements.
p12:11|          Project Scope Elements
p12:12|       The core of the Project is roadway widening to add 12 passing lanes within 5 designated
p12:13|       segments of highway between Pueblo and the Kansas state line. The passing lanes shall be of
p12:14|       optimal length and adhere to grant commitments while maximizing safety and minimizing
p12:15|       inconvenience to the traveling public. Minimal design has been completed. The CM, Design
p12:16|       Consultant, the ICE, and CDOT will work collaboratively to determine the optimal location of
p12:17|       each passing lane within the segments, design, and package the passing lanes through final
p12:18|       design.
p12:19|       The 12 locations and five segments of US 50 are as follows:
p12:20|             Segment                         Number of Passing Lanes
p12:21|Pueblo to Fowler                        2 (1 EB, 1 WB)
p12:22|Fowler to Manzanola                     1 (1 EB)
p12:23|La Junta to Las Animas                  3 (1 EB, 2 WB)
p12:24|Las Animas to Lamar                     4 (2 EB, 2 WB)
p12:25|Lamar to Kansas Border                  2 (1 EB, 1 WB)
p12:26|
p12:27|       The proposed packaging is to include up to 6 passing lanes with the lowest risk (e.g., minimal
p12:28|       ROW required, limited utility conflicts, less extensive environmental review) into Package 1 and
p12:29|       then the remaining passing lanes into Package 2.
